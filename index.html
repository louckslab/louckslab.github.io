From genetic variation to
better pain management

Severe pain during childhood has lasting effects on the development of a child. This is why we study how genetic variation shapes pain treatment response. By integrating pharmacogenomics (pharmacology + genomics), model organism genetics (using the roundworm Caenorhabditis elegans), and deep patient partnership, we work toward personalized pain management that is safer and more effective for every child.

What We Study

Our approach to personalized pain management

Our work follows a Discovery → Validation → Implementation pipeline — from identifying genetic predictors of individual drug responses in children and determmining their biological relevance to paiun management using C. elegans, to translating findings into clinical policy through patient partnership.

1
Discovery

Identifying Genetic Predictors of Individual Pain Treatment Responses

Using genomic studies considering large populations of children, our team maps the DNA variations that can help predict how likely a child is to develop painful conditions, experience serious pain, and respond to a variety of pain relievers, including opioids. Identification of the specific genetic cause lays the foundation for which the rest of the pipeline is built upon.

2
Validation

Replicating Findings and Establishing Causal Evidence

We validate discoveries by replicating results in additional populations of children and performing functional testing in the roundworm C. elegans to establish causal evidence linking genetic variation to individual pain treatment responses.

3
Implementation

Translating Findings to Clinical Policy

Through our extensive involvement with patient partners and the Canadian Pharmacogenomics Network for Drug Safety (CPNDS), we aim to bring genetic findings to the clinic — shaping clinician guidelines and regulatory policy for safer, personalized pain management.

Patient Partners with Lived Experiences

We partner directly with patients and families to ensure our pain research is driven by real-world needs. Our patient partners help shape the questions we ask, ensuring our work reflects the needs and voices of the people it is meant to serve.

Cancer access research collaborator
Suad Ismail, BSW

Suad Ismail, BSW is a cancer access research collaborator, patient partner, and childhood osteogenic sarcoma survivor dedicated to improving equitable access to cancer care. Diagnosed with osteogenic sarcoma at age 13, her lived experience has shaped a career focused on patient-oriented research, health system improvement, and advocacy. With over 14 years of experience in social work, Suad brings expertise in trauma-informed practice, family support, and navigating complex healthcare systems.

She collaborates with researchers across Canada to advance patient-centered cancer research and knowledge translation, with a particular interest in improving timely diagnosis and reducing barriers to care. Through public speaking, research partnerships, and advocacy, Suad works to ensure that patient experiences inform healthcare policy, clinical practice, and research.

Suad is passionate about transforming lived experience into meaningful change for children, adolescents, and families affected by cancer.

Administrative Assistant (Provincial Home-Based Care, PEI)
Caregiver Partner Advocate
Robyn Wood

Robyn Wood, AA, is a dedicated advocate for individuals and families affected by cancer. As the mother of a cancer survivor, she brings both personal experience and a deep commitment to advancing patient- and family-centered care. Robyn actively volunteers with the IWK Health Centre and has served as a Patient and Family Advisor on the OncoFertility Project Committee. In recognition of her contributions to patient engagement, she was selected to participate in the Patient Involvement in Cancer Research (PIP) Program at the 2025 Canadian Cancer Research Conference (CCRC), where she represented the patient and family perspective in discussions supporting cancer research and knowledge advancement. She also serves as a Board Member with her local Communities Kids Committee, contributing to initiatives that promote the health, wellbeing, and development of children and youth.

In her professional role, Robyn serves as an Administrative Assistant with Home-Based Care at Health PEI, providing administrative leadership and support for provincial programs, committees, and strategic initiatives. Recognized for her exceptional organizational skills, attention to detail, and commitment to excellence, she plays an integral role in facilitating collaboration and supporting the effective delivery of home-based care services across the province.

Through both her professional responsibilities and volunteer involvement, Robyn remains committed to enhancing healthcare experiences, strengthening patient and family engagement, and contributing to improved outcomes for individuals and communities across Prince Edward Island and beyond.
