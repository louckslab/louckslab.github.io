From genetic variation to
better pain management

Severe pain during childhood has lasting effects on the development of a child. This is why we study how genetic variation shapes pain treatment response. By integrating pharmacology, whole genome analysis, C. elegans functional validation, and deep patient partnership, we work toward personalized pain management that is safer and more effective for every child.

What We Study

Our approach to personalized pain management

Our work follows a Discovery → Validation → Implementation pipeline — from identifying genetic predictors of drug response and functionally validating them in C. elegans, to translating findings into clinical policy through patient partnership.

1
Discovery

Identifying Genetic Predictors of Drug Response

Using cohort genomic studies, our team maps the DNA variations that cause children to have adverse reactions to opioid medications. Identification of the specific genetic cause lays the foundation for which the rest of the pipeline is built upon.

2
Validation

Replicating Results and Establishing Causal Evidence

We validate discoveries by replicating results in additional patient populations and performing functional testing in C. elegans nematode worms to establish causal evidence linking genetic variants to drug responses.

3
Implementation

Translating Findings to Clinical Policy

Through our extensive involvement with patient partners and the Canadian Pharmacogenomics Network for Drug Safety (CPNDS), we aim to bring genetic findings to the clinic — shaping clinician guidelines and regulatory policy for safer, personalized pain management.
